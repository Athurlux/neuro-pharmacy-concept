# Visual provenance and limitations

All sixteen bitmap masters were made with the built-in image generation tool. Prompts are in GENERATION_PROMPTS.json. SVG branding, icons and process diagrams were authored as editable vector assets. Social covers combine generated imagery with deterministic typography.

The photographic sign motifs vary between brain and leaf shapes. Those are incidental scene graphics, not a verified official identity, and do not establish neurological specialisation. Use the supplied typographic wordmark as the consistent website identity. No staff biographies or real names should be attached to generated faces. The two delivery compositions are separate illustrative scenes, not documentary views of one branch or necessarily the same rider.

The desktop delivery image includes a fictional plate and motorbike markings; do not extract them into real business facts. The consultation image contains decorative health-related signage, which must not become offered-service or efficacy claims. No driving animation or emergency delivery promise is implied.

Products have intentionally blank labels. Do not infer ingredients, volume, strength, manufacturer, child suitability, antibacterial action or device accuracy. The first aid pouch is empty; do not call it a stocked medical kit. The thermometer has no diagnostic display. Backgrounds are opaque off-white, not transparent cutouts; match the card canvas rather than pretending alpha exists.

Main failure risks: misleading public ordering, fake branch details, product claims invented from artwork, or real patient information captured by a demo. Keep the concept honest while making every frontend journey complete. The visual pack does not substitute for verified inventory, brand approval or a production data-handling system.
