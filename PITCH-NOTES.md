# Pitch notes: Neuro Pharmacy website concept

Talking points for presenting the concept. The aim is to show what a clearer website could do, not to claim measured results.

## First-time understanding

- The first screen answers "what can I do here?" in live text, with three actions (find a branch, ask about an item, shop everyday essentials) plus a link to delivery. A new visitor doesn't need to know the business already.
- The quick enquiry sits on the hero's counter edge: choose a branch, type an item, continue. It turns the most common pharmacy question, "do you have this?", into the first interaction.
- On phones the photo becomes a short band, so the headline, actions and enquiry come first and a tall portrait doesn't push them below the fold.
- The visual identity stays calm and neighbourly: teal, mint and warm white, one typeface, real-feeling people. It avoids the clinical look and avoids suggesting neurological specialisation because of the name.

## Why branch discovery is prominent

- Public listings disagree about where Neuro's branches are and which numbers to call. A website is the natural place to settle that, and every journey (enquiry, pharmacist contact, collection, delivery) depends on choosing a branch.
- The concept shows each branch honestly: what the sources say, which numbers were found, and what must be confirmed. This is a ready-made checklist for the business, and it shows that the build was done carefully.
- A chosen branch follows the visitor into enquiries and checkout, so they don't have to pick it again.

## Showing functionality with the basket and delivery journey

- A real shopping flow runs end to end: browse with search, filters and sort; open product detail; add and merge items; change quantities in a slide-out panel or on the basket page; choose delivery or collection; enter details; review and edit; and get a confirmation with a reference and printable summary.
- Totals stay consistent across the header, panel, basket, checkout and confirmation. The fee changes visibly between delivery and collection. A double click can't complete an order twice, and an empty basket can't be checked out.
- The delivery page leads with the rider, motorbike and marked box, so it reads as pharmacy delivery before any text. It promises nothing about coverage or timing and says so.
- Medicine questions are kept apart from the shop on purpose. That is how a pharmacy should handle them, and it is a safe way to demonstrate online ordering.

## What an official launch would require

1. **Verified branches and contacts:** confirmed addresses, numbers, hours and services for each branch, plus map locations once they're confirmed.
2. **Approved brand and content:** Neuro's real logo and wording, real photography of staff and premises with consent, and review of every health-related statement.
3. **A real catalogue and order handling:** actual products, prices and stock rules, payment options (card, mobile money and cash on delivery are different things), delivery zones and fees, and a way for branches to receive and confirm orders.
4. **An appropriate prescription process:** how originals are checked, what may be requested online, pharmacist review, and the professional and regulatory requirements that apply.
5. **Data protection:** handling of personal and health-related information in line with Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act, with secure storage, retention rules and consent.

## Practical next step

A short call with the owner to confirm the branch list and decide which first release matters most: branch directory and enquiries only, or ordering as well. The design is built to support either.
